01 • The basics

So what is celiac disease?

Celiac disease is a long-term autoimmune condition. It is not just a food preference or an allergy.

In someone with celiac disease, eating gluten causes the immune system to react. That reaction damages the lining of the small intestine, where nutrients are absorbed. Over time, this can lead to digestive symptoms, nutrient deficiencies, and problems in other parts of the body.

The condition is often linked to genes, but having those genes does not automatically mean someone will develop it. It is also different from wheat allergy or non-celiac gluten sensitivity.

Important distinction

Celiac disease is not the same as a simple intolerance or a mild dislike of gluten. It is a medical condition that needs proper evaluation.

What gluten is

Gluten is a protein found in wheat, barley, and rye. It is common in bread, pasta, cereal, and many processed foods.

02 • Symptoms

Symptoms can look very different from person to person.

Some people have obvious digestive symptoms. Others feel tired, unwell, or have issues that are harder to connect to the gut.

  • Diarrhea or constipation
  • Bloating or gas
  • Stomach pain
  • Nausea or vomiting
  • Fatigue
  • Iron deficiency anemia
  • Weight changes
  • Skin rash
  • Bone or joint pain
  • Growth concerns in children

Not everyone has digestive symptoms. Someone may feel tired, have low iron, or notice mood or skin changes before the diagnosis is made.

That is why symptoms alone are not enough. A healthcare professional needs to assess the full picture.

03 • Diagnosis

Diagnosis takes proper testing.

Testing is most reliable while the person is still eating gluten, unless a doctor advises otherwise.

01

Blood tests

Doctors often start with celiac antibodies, such as tTG-IgA and total IgA.

02

Endoscopy

An upper endoscopy may be used to take biopsies from the small intestine for examination.

03

Context matters

Symptoms, blood results, and medical history are all considered together before a diagnosis is made.

04 • Treatment

There is no cure yet, but there is treatment.

The standard treatment is a lifelong gluten-free diet, guided by healthcare advice and regular monitoring.

Remove gluten

Following a careful gluten-free diet is the foundation of treatment.

Check ingredients

Read labels and be mindful of cross-contact in kitchens and restaurants.

Monitor health

Regular follow-up can help check symptoms, nutrition, and healing over time.
